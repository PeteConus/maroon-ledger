What moved the bottom line?

Each Budget update reconciles the new forecast for the net operating balance against the last one, line by line.

Some lines are the government's own decisions: new spending and revenue measures. Others come from outside its direct control: tax receipts running ahead or behind, coal prices moving royalties, the GST pool being shared differently, Commonwealth disaster funding arriving in a different year. Reading the two together is a fairer way to judge a Budget than looking at the surplus or deficit alone, whoever is in government.

How we group the lines

The line items and figures are exactly as published. The grouping into “government decisions” and “economy and other revisions” is ours. We count revenue measures, expense measures and the additional enterprise bargaining provision as decisions; everything else, including “other parameter adjustments”, as revisions. Treasury notes that other parameter adjustments include Commonwealth funding revisions, flows from government-owned corporations, interest, depreciation, lapses and deferrals, so a small part of that line may reflect earlier decisions.
